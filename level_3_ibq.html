Leather, Earth & Green Peat

Isobutyl Quinoline (IBQ / Pyralone)

The legendary molecule of rebellious 20th-century perfumery: bitter tanned hide, green walnut husks, and damp mossy peat.

What Does IBQ Bring to the Table?

1. The Castoreum Connection

IBQ shares profound chemical and olfactory bridges with Castoreum (the beaver musk used in vintage leather). Both contain phenolic, pyrazinic, and quinoline notes that mimic smoked birch bark, warm animal fur, and cured tanned hides!

2. The Secret "Mossy-Earthy" Crossover

IBQ is not just a dry jacket. It is deeply mossy, damp-soil, and peat-like. That is why it acts as a dark anchor directly complementing or replacing Evernyl in Chypres.

3. Green Walnut & Bitter Vetiver

It has a piercing astringent green facet (like biting into a raw green walnut rind) that cuts sweet resins like a razor blade.

4. ⚠️ The 1% Dilution Mandate

At 100%, IBQ smells like burnt tires, diesel, and harsh acrid smoke. On the student bench, it must always be worked at 1% or 10% dilution. A single micro-drop at 1% provides massive, elegant leather sillage!

The Multi-Facet Reality of IBQ / Pyralone

Never treat IBQ as just "leather". It is a multifaceted green-earth powerhouse:

Facet 1 (Dominant)
Cured Saddle Leather

Bitter, dry, aristocratic cured hide and worn motorcycle jacket.

Facet 2 (The Chypre Secret)
Damp Peat & Lichen Moss

Earthy forest soil and oakmoss dampness that anchors classical Chypres.

Facet 3 (Astringent)
Bitter Green Walnut Rind

Crisp, vegetal, snappy green bitterness that slices through sweet balsams.

Facet 4 (Smoke)
Birch Wood Tar Smoke

Subtle Russian leather campfire smoke and cured hide resins.
