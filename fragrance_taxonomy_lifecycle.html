🔬 Odor Classification & Description

Based on the TGSC visual vocabulary framework. Maps scent concepts and materials.

Floral & Fruits
🌸 Floral (rose, jasmine) 🍋 Citrus (lemon, bergamot) 🍓 Fruit (berry, tropical)
Subgroup Overlap: Citrus fruits (like Lemon) technically fall under the broader Fruity umbrella. However, citrus flowers (like Neroli) lean heavily Floral, and citrus leaves (like Petitgrain) overlap with Green, despite their shared botanical origin.
Woody & Earthy
🪵 Woody (sandal, cedar) 🪨 Earthy (patchouli, soil) 🦠 Moss / Lichen (oakmoss)
Green & Herbal
🥬 Green (leaf, grass) 🌿 Herbal (mint, sage) 🌾 Hay (coumarin, tonka)
Gourmand & Spicy
🍦 Vanilla / Choc / Coffee 🍬 Sweet (caramel, honey) 🌶️ Spicy (pepper, clove) 🌾 Hay (coumarin, tonka)
Savory & Unconventional (TGSC Extensions)
🧅 Allium (garlic, onion) 🍖 Meaty / Savory 🌋 Sulfurous
Intersection Example: Is Coumarin/Tonka Bean Gourmand (almond/sweet) or Herbal (fresh hay)? It bridges both classifications, forming the backbone of the Fougère archetype.

🏛️ Fragrance Archetypes

Combinations of odors and raw materials defining classical landmark styles.

Fougère (Lavender/Oakmoss/Coumarin) Chypre (Citrus/Labdanum/Oakmoss) Amber (Vanilla/Resins/Woods) Cologne (Citrus/Herbal/Neroli) Gourmand (Edible/Dessert-like)
Archetypes serve as structural blueprints during Mind-mapping and Formulation, combining specific classifications into recognizable cultural patterns.

🎡 Fragrance Family

Based on Michael Edwards' Fragrance Wheel, describing the overall tone of a finished fragrance.

Floral Soft Floral Floral Amber Soft Amber Amber Woody Amber Woods Mossy Woods Dry Woods Aromatic Fougère Citrus Water Green Fruity
Families provide high-level consumer-facing communication. Often defined in the Briefing and verified during Evaluation.

🔄 Lifecycle Integration Matrix

How each taxonomy is utilized across the fragrance development lifecycle.

Briefing

  • Families: Used to communicate high-level marketing goals (e.g., "We need a new Woody Amber").
  • Archetypes: Sometimes mentioned to set structural expectations (e.g., "A modern Fougère").
  • Odor Class: Rarely used directly, unless specific hero notes are requested (e.g., "Must feature a strong rosy note").

Objective

  • Families: Anchors the final target market and tone.
  • Archetypes: Defines the core technical blueprint to be modified by the perfumer.
  • Odor Class: Establishes specific nuances (e.g., "The top note must be sharp and green, not fruity").

Mind-mapping

Branching out from the main idea(s) set in the objective. It helps translate the perfume objective into something that has a structure—not in exact percentages like a formula, but in terms of feeling and interconnected nodes.

  • Archetypes: Used to brainstorm structural twists (e.g., "What if we take a Chypre but replace the citrus with tropical fruit?").
  • Odor Class: Heavily used to cluster raw materials by facet to conceptualize new accords.

Formulation

  • Archetypes: The actual skeleton being built in the lab (the ratio of base materials).
  • Odor Class: The primary tool for selecting specific raw materials (e.g., "I need a balsamic material to soften the dry woods").

Testing/Evaluation

  • Families: Evaluated for market fit: Does it fit the intended consumer family? Is it recognizable as a 'Citrus'?
  • Odor Class: Used to evaluate the performance and evaporation curve of specific facets over time (e.g., "The spicy facet fades too quickly on skin").
