Evernyl (Veramoss / Methyl Atrarate)

The clean lichen bark and damp forest shadow that cuts sweet notes and anchors the classic Chypre and Fougère.

Why Compose with Synthetic Evernyl?

1. The Exact IFRA Standard on Moss Extracts

Under IFRA Standards, restrictions for Oakmoss and Treemoss extracts are directly linked to the presence of Atranol and Chloroatranol. Furthermore, IFRA mandates a cumulative limit: if Treemoss is used alongside Oakmoss, the permitted level must be reduced accordingly so the combined total does not exceed the category maximum.

2. Unrestricted Dosage Freedom with Evernyl

Because Evernyl (Methyl atrarate) is a single defined aroma chemical free from atranol/chloroatranol, it is not bound by the moss extract cumulative ceiling. Perfumers can dose it freely at 1%, 2%, or 5%+ to create modern, room-filling mossy sillage.

3. The Sweet Spun-Sugar Secret

Unlike bitter natural absolute, synthetic Evernyl has an intense medicinal caramel and spun-sugar sweetness at higher doses, making it versatile across both vintage chypres and modern gourmands.

4. The IBQ / Leather Bridge

Evernyl's damp-peat facet locks seamlessly into Isobutyl Quinoline (IBQ), forming the dark earthy spine of iconic leather chypres (*Bandit, Cabochard*).

The Multi-Facet Reality of Evernyl (Veramoss)

Never treat Evernyl as just "bitter moss". It is a multi-dimensional sweet-moss paradox:

Facet 1 (Dominant)
Damp Lichen & Oak Bark

Clean, dry gray-green tree lichen and forest floor bark.

Facet 2 (The Paradox)
Spun Sugar & Cotton Candy

Intensely sweet medicinal caramel facet that emerges at higher dosages.

Facet 3 (Mineral)
Saline Sea-Breeze Air

Subtle marine-salt air clinging to coastal oak trees.

Facet 4 (The Crossover)
Damp Peat Soil (IBQ Bond)

Rich earthy dampness that locks with Isobutyl Quinoline in leather chypres.

1 What is this sensation? (The Sweet Moss Paradox)

Many textbooks mistakenly describe Evernyl as purely dry or bitter. That is only half the story!

"Evernyl holds a famous sensory paradox: while it brings the earthy, lichen-bark texture of an oak tree, it is also intensely sweet. At higher dosages, it reveals a distinctive spun-sugar, medicinal caramel, and cotton-candy facet."

This sweet-saline-moss duality is precisely why Francis Kurkdjian overdosed Evernyl in Baccarat Rouge 540 alongside Ethyl Maltol. It is not cold, dead ash—it is a warm, sweet, mineral-moss crystal that clings to skin like velvet!
