The Botanical Razor

Green Leaf & Grass (Cis-3-Hexenol)

The electric snap of freshly broken stems and dew on cut grass that cuts through sweetness and injects living realism.

Why Compose Green Notes from Raw Materials?

1. The "Cooked Spinach" Problem

You cannot steam-distill fresh grass or flower leaves! The heat cooks the leaves, turning the fresh green snap into smelly brown compost or boiled cabbage.

2. Isolating the Living Plant Signal

When a plant leaf is damaged, its enzymes instantly synthesize Cis-3-Hexenol (the "Leaf Alcohol"). Perfumers synthesize this exact molecule to capture the instantaneous snap of a crushed stem.

3. The Sweetness Scissors

Perfumes with only flowers and vanilla quickly become cloying and sugary. A trace micro-drop of green functions like scissors, trimming the sugar and making flowers smell freshly picked from a garden.

4. ⚠️ The 1% Dilution Mandate

Pure Cis-3-Hexenol is violently powerful. At 100%, 1 drop will ruin an entire bottle with a pungent petroleum-grass stench. It MUST always be pre-diluted to 1% or 0.1% on your organ!

The Multi-Facet Reality of Cis-3-Hexenol

Cis-3-Hexenol is not just "lawn mowing". It has multifaceted botanical facets:

Facet 1 (Dominant)
Broken Green Stem

Crisp, watery, electric snap of breaking a dandelion stem in spring.

Facet 2 (Fruity)
Green Apple Skin

Tart, mouth-watering Granny Smith apple peel and unripe green banana.

Facet 3 (Floral)
Rose Calyx & Violet Leaf

The cool dewy green leaves and thorny green calyx cupping the rose petals.

Facet 4 (Atmospheric)
Rain on Cut Grass

The ozonic, petrichor chill of a rain shower falling on a freshly mowed lawn.
